# AdSpolisher --- Business Model, Services, Pricing & Website MVP

**Document type:** Product and business planning brief\
**Status:** Initial strategy hypothesis --- validate with customers
before committing to full development\
**Domain:** `adspolisher.com`\
**Market ambition:** Global\
**Business model:** Service-led marketing business, supported by AI and
manual expertise\
**Initial payment model:** Pay per project / fixed-price packages /
custom quotes\
**Subscription:** Optional future offering, not required for launch

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Executive summary

AdSpolisher should launch as a **marketing service business**, not as a
self-serve AI SaaS product.

Customers pay AdSpolisher to help improve their marketing and
advertising. Depending on the customer's needs, the work may include ad
creative production, copywriting, campaign strategy, landing-page
recommendations, campaign setup, and ongoing campaign optimization.

AI should help the team work faster, explore creative directions, and
analyze information. Human review remains important for strategy,
factual accuracy, brand fit, creative quality, and campaign decisions.

The website's job is to explain the services, build trust, show
examples, capture qualified leads, and let customers buy clearly defined
packages or request a custom quote.

**Core principle:** Sell a clear business outcome and defined
deliverables---not vague promises of "AI marketing" or guaranteed sales
growth.

## 2. Business positioning

### Proposed positioning statement

> AdSpolisher helps businesses create, improve, and manage digital
> advertising through a combination of marketing expertise, creative
> production, and AI-assisted workflows.

### Short value proposition

> Better ad creative. Clearer strategy. Smarter campaign execution.

This positioning is broad enough to accommodate a complete marketing
service, but the first website release should emphasize a small set of
services the team can reliably deliver.

### What AdSpolisher should not promise

-   Guaranteed sales, leads, ROAS, or revenue growth.
-   Guaranteed ad approval by Meta, Google, or another platform.
-   Performance claims that are not supported by real client data.
-   Services or expertise that the team cannot currently provide.
-   "Fully automated" marketing if important work still requires human
    review.

Marketing results depend on factors such as product-market fit, offer,
price, audience, creative, landing page, competition, budget, tracking,
and execution.

## 3. Target customers

The ambition is global, but "any business globally" is too broad to be a
useful initial targeting strategy.

### Initial customer hypothesis

Start by testing demand among:

-   Small and medium-sized businesses that need help with digital
    advertising.
-   E-commerce and direct-to-consumer brands that regularly need product
    ads.
-   Service businesses that want leads, inquiries, or bookings.
-   Founders who do not have an in-house marketing team.

Agencies and freelancers may become a later customer segment if
AdSpolisher can provide reliable fulfillment, volume pricing,
white-label reports, or collaboration features.

### Initial geography and language

-   Use English as the initial website language.
-   Make the site understandable to customers in multiple countries.
-   Consider USD display pricing for international customers, while
    making clear what currency is charged at checkout.
-   Verify payment-provider availability, supported countries, taxes,
    and business requirements before accepting international payments.
-   Do not assume one global price will work for every market. Test
    packages and demand before introducing region-specific pricing.

### Important decision

Choose one primary customer segment for the first marketing experiment,
even if the website can accept inquiries from other businesses. The
first segment should be the one the founder can reach and serve most
credibly.

## 4. Business model

AdSpolisher should begin with **service revenue**. Customers pay for
work that is agreed in advance.

### Model A --- Fixed-price packages

A customer selects a defined service, sees what is included, submits a
brief, and pays or starts checkout.

This is best for repeatable deliverables such as an ad audit, an ad-copy
package, or a set of creative assets.

### Model B --- Custom projects

For larger or less predictable requirements, a customer submits a
project brief. AdSpolisher reviews the scope, gives a quote, agrees on
deliverables and timeline, and requests payment or a deposit.

This is appropriate for full campaign strategy, multi-channel work, and
complex creative projects.

### Model C --- Ongoing campaign management

A customer pays an agreed recurring fee for work delivered over a
defined period, such as monthly campaign management or a set number of
new creative assets.

This is a **service retainer**, not necessarily a software subscription.
It should be offered only if the team can deliver ongoing work reliably.

### Recommended starting model

1.  Launch with fixed-price packages for work that can be standardized.
2.  Offer custom quotes for complete marketing projects.
3.  Add ongoing retainers after confirming the team can fulfill the
    recurring scope.
4.  Do not build subscription billing or a self-serve AI platform just
    because it is common in software products.

### Example customer journey

1.  Customer visits `adspolisher.com`.
2.  Customer reviews services, examples, deliverables, and indicative
    prices.
3.  Customer selects a package or submits a project brief.
4.  AdSpolisher confirms the scope, requirements, timeline, and price.
5.  Customer pays in full or pays an agreed deposit.
6.  The team delivers the work and handles the agreed revision process.
7.  Customer approves the delivery and may order another project or
    request ongoing help.

## 5. Initial services to offer

The following is a proposed menu. Before publishing it, confirm that the
founder and team can deliver each service to a professional standard. If
not, remove it or label it as a future service.

### Service 1 --- Ad Audit & Improvement

**Customer problem:** Existing ads are unclear, repetitive, or not
generating the desired response.

**Possible deliverables:** - Review of a defined number of ads. -
Assessment of hook, audience fit, benefit clarity, offer, proof, call to
action, and creative-message consistency. - Prioritized recommendations
with explanations. - Rewritten copy or headline variations. - A
practical testing plan.

**Pricing hypothesis:** US\$29--\$99 per small audit, depending on depth
and number of ads.

### Service 2 --- Ad Creative & Copy Package

**Customer problem:** The business needs new ad concepts and usable
creative assets.

**Possible deliverables:** - A short creative brief. - A defined number
of creative concepts. - Ad headlines and primary text. - Design assets
in agreed formats and dimensions. - A specified number of revision
rounds. - Final export files.

**Pricing hypothesis:** US\$99--\$399 per package, depending on asset
count, complexity, formats, and revisions.

### Service 3 --- Campaign Strategy

**Customer problem:** The business needs a clearer plan before investing
in advertising.

**Possible deliverables:** - Business and offer review. - Target
audience hypotheses. - Messaging and creative angles. - Recommended
campaign structure. - Testing priorities and success metrics. -
Landing-page and conversion-path recommendations.

**Pricing hypothesis:** US\$199--\$799 per project, depending on scope
and research required.

### Service 4 --- Campaign Setup & Management

**Customer problem:** The business needs practical help implementing and
improving paid campaigns.

**Possible deliverables, when within the team's expertise:** - Campaign
setup and configuration. - Tracking and measurement checks. - Creative
and copy implementation. - Agreed monitoring and optimization cadence. -
Periodic performance report and recommendations.

**Pricing approach:** Custom setup fee and/or a monthly service
retainer. The customer's ad spend should be clearly separate from
AdSpolisher's service fee.

### Do not launch every service by default

Start with the two services the team can deliver best and repeatedly. A
focused offering is easier to explain, price, fulfill, and improve.

### How to set final prices

For each package, estimate:

-   Human work hours.
-   AI/API and software costs.
-   Design, stock asset, and production costs.
-   Payment processing and currency conversion fees.
-   Expected revision time.
-   Project management and customer support.
-   Taxes and other operating costs.
-   Desired contribution and profit.

Use a simple internal calculation:

`Price − direct delivery costs − payment fees = contribution before overhead and tax`

Do not price solely by the number of AI prompts or generated images.
Price the defined deliverable, expertise, time saved, complexity, and
value to the customer.

### Package rules

Every paid package should clearly state:

-   Exactly what is included.
-   What is not included.
-   Number and format of deliverables.
-   Required customer inputs.
-   Delivery timeline and when the timeline begins.
-   Number of revision rounds.
-   Whether editable/source files are included.
-   Whether ad spend, platform fees, or third-party assets are included.
-   Cancellation, refund, and project-pausing terms.
-   What happens if the customer delays providing information.

For custom projects, use a written scope and a payment schedule. A
deposit may be appropriate before work begins.

## 7. Website MVP

The first version should be a polished service-business website with
lead capture and a straightforward way to purchase or request services.
It does not need a full SaaS dashboard.

### Required pages

#### 1. Home

Purpose: Explain the value quickly and direct visitors to a service or
quote request.

Suggested sections: - Hero with clear positioning. - Primary CTA:
"Explore Services" or "Get a Quote." - Main service categories. - How
the process works. - Selected portfolio examples. - Reasons to work with
AdSpolisher, supported by evidence. - Testimonials or case studies only
when genuine and authorized. - FAQ and final CTA.

#### 2. Services

Show each active service with: - The customer problem. - What
AdSpolisher does. - Deliverables. - Who the service is for. - Starting
price or "Request a Quote." - CTA to purchase or submit a brief.

#### 3. Portfolio / Case Studies

Show examples of actual work with permission.

For each case study, explain: - Customer context and goal. - The work
performed. - The deliverables. - The result, if measured and verified. -
Relevant time period and limitations.

If there are no client projects yet, use clearly labeled concept work or
sample projects. Never present fictional results as client outcomes.

#### 4. How It Works

A simple process: 1. Tell us about your business and goal. 2. Agree on
scope, deliverables, and price. 3. Provide assets and access only when
needed. 4. AdSpolisher completes the work. 5. Review the delivery and
use the agreed revision rounds.

#### 5. Get a Quote / Start a Project

Suggested fields: - Name. - Work email. - Business name and website. -
Country/time zone. - Service required. - Advertising platform, if
relevant. - Business goal. - Budget range. - Desired deadline. - Project
description. - Optional links or uploads. - Consent and privacy notice.

Collect only the information needed to assess the project. Do not
request passwords or ad-account credentials through a general form.

#### 6. Contact

Provide a real business contact channel and a clear expected response
time. Use genuine business details.

#### 7. Legal and policy pages

Before accepting payments, prepare: - Privacy Policy. - Terms of
Service. - Service-specific scope and delivery terms. - Cancellation and
refund policy. - Cookie disclosures or consent controls where
required. - Business identity and contact information appropriate to the
operating jurisdiction.

Get suitable legal advice for the actual countries served and the
business's location.

### Optional later pages

-   Insights / blog.
-   Individual service landing pages.
-   Client portal.
-   Order tracking.
-   Downloadable reports.
-   Customer dashboard.

Build these only when they support a demonstrated business need.

## 8. Recommended order and payment flow

### For a fixed-price package

1.  Customer selects a package.
2.  Customer reviews deliverables, exclusions, timeline, and revision
    terms.
3.  Customer completes a project brief.
4.  Customer pays through a supported payment provider.
5.  The system confirms the order and sends a receipt or confirmation.
6.  The team reviews the brief and requests missing information if
    needed.
7.  The team delivers the work through a secure, agreed channel.
8.  The customer receives the included revisions and completion
    instructions.

If the scope still needs discussion, collect a quote request first
rather than charging an unclear price.

### For a custom project

1.  Customer submits the brief.
2.  AdSpolisher reviews feasibility and requirements.
3.  The team sends a written quote and scope.
4.  Both sides agree on deliverables and timeline.
5.  Customer pays the agreed deposit or project amount.
6.  Work begins after payment and required inputs are received.

### Payment-provider checklist

Select a provider based on: - The business's country of registration. -
International card and currency support. - Payout eligibility. -
Processing and currency conversion fees. - Refund and dispute
handling. - Tax and invoicing needs. - Supported service categories and
compliance requirements.

Do not assume that a particular provider supports every country or
business setup.

## 9. How AI and manual work fit together

AI is a delivery tool, not the entire business proposition.

### Suitable AI-assisted tasks

-   First drafts of ad copy and headlines.
-   Brainstorming creative angles.
-   Summarizing customer briefs.
-   Structuring audit reports.
-   Generating variations for human review.
-   Drafting campaign plans and report summaries.
-   Helping organize research and feedback.

### Human review responsibilities

-   Confirm facts, offers, product claims, and brand voice.
-   Check creative quality and platform requirements.
-   Review strategy and campaign setup.
-   Validate customer-facing deliverables.
-   Approve performance conclusions.
-   Obtain customer approval for sensitive or high-impact changes.

Do not send fabricated testimonials, unsupported claims, misleading
offers, or unverified performance promises to customers. AI output
should be reviewed before delivery.

## 10. Technical approach

The initial site can use a simple modern web stack. Avoid building
infrastructure that does not support the service workflow.

### Suggested stack

-   **Frontend:** Next.js, React, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS.
-   **Backend:** Next.js server-side routes/actions or a small Node.js
    API.
-   **Database:** PostgreSQL with Prisma, if storing leads and orders in
    a database is useful.
-   **Forms:** Validated server-side form submission with spam
    protection.
-   **Payments:** A supported hosted checkout provider, added after
    packages and terms are defined.
-   **Email:** Transactional email for inquiry acknowledgments, quotes,
    and order updates.
-   **File handling:** Secure uploads only when needed; validate file
    types, size limits, access, and retention.
-   **Analytics:** Privacy-conscious traffic and conversion measurement.
-   **Operations:** Error monitoring, backups, access controls, and
    secrets stored outside source control.

### Minimum data model, if an application backend is needed

-   `Lead`: contact details, company, requested service, project
    description, status.
-   `ServicePackage`: name, description, price/currency, deliverables,
    active status.
-   `Order`: customer/lead reference, scope, amount, currency, payment
    status, delivery status.
-   `ProjectFile`: file metadata and secure storage reference, where
    uploads are supported.
-   `ProjectUpdate`: status, customer communications, and delivery
    milestones.

Do not build authentication, a customer dashboard, complex role systems,
or a multi-tenant SaaS architecture unless the actual workflow requires
them.

## 11. Customer acquisition plan

The first goal is not global traffic. It is to win a small number of
suitable customers and learn how to serve them well.

### Initial channels

1.  **Direct outreach:** Contact businesses whose needs match the
    service. Personalize each message and do not claim knowledge of
    their campaign performance without evidence.
2.  **Portfolio:** Publish strong sample work and authorized client
    projects.
3.  **Partnerships:** Build relationships with web developers, brand
    designers, freelancers, and consultants who may refer customers.
4.  **Useful content:** Publish practical advertising examples, creative
    breakdowns, and improvement guides that demonstrate expertise.
5.  **Search:** Create focused service pages around the real services
    delivered, rather than publishing generic AI-generated content at
    scale.
6.  **Referrals:** Ask satisfied customers for honest reviews and
    introductions.

### Initial validation target

Over the first 30 days: - Interview 10--15 prospective customers. -
Review competitor service packages and terms. - Create 3--5 strong
sample deliverables. - Publish the MVP website. - Contact 30--50
carefully selected potential customers. - Aim to secure 3 paid pilot
projects, if the offer and outreach generate sufficient interest. -
Record delivery time, customer feedback, direct costs, revision effort,
and repeat-work requests.

These are operating targets, not guaranteed results or industry
benchmarks.

## 12. Key metrics

Track: - Qualified inquiries. - Inquiry-to-quote conversion. - Quote
acceptance rate. - Paid projects won. - Average project value. - Time
and cost to fulfill each service. - Revision frequency and reasons. -
On-time delivery rate. - Customer satisfaction and referrals. - Repeat
purchases or retainer conversions. - Contribution per project.

Do not evaluate success only through visits, social followers, or the
number of AI-generated assets.

## 14. 30-day execution plan

### Week 1 --- Define and validate

-   Confirm the exact services the founder can deliver.
-   Choose the first customer segment.
-   Review competitor service pages and packages.
-   Interview potential customers.
-   Define the first two packages and their deliverables.

**Deliverable:** A clear service menu and initial pricing hypotheses.

### Week 2 --- Create proof and design

-   Create sample ads, audits, and strategy deliverables.
-   Prepare the visual identity and website structure.
-   Write the home, services, process, portfolio, FAQ, and contact
    content.
-   Define lead qualification and project intake.

**Deliverable:** Approved content and a usable website prototype.

### Week 3 --- Build the MVP

-   Implement responsive marketing pages.
-   Add quote and contact forms.
-   Add service details and portfolio examples.
-   Configure email notifications and basic analytics.
-   Prepare legal pages and payment flow appropriate to the business
    setup.

**Deliverable:** A tested website ready for initial customers.

### Week 4 --- Launch and learn

-   Start personalized outreach.
-   Follow up on qualified inquiries.
-   Deliver the first paid projects.
-   Record fulfillment costs and feedback.
-   Improve the packages and website based on real conversations.

**Deliverable:** Early customer evidence and a prioritized improvement
backlog.

## 15. Decisions to make before development

The founder should explicitly answer:

-   Which two services can we deliver best today?
-   Who will do the creative, strategy, and campaign work?
-   Which customer segment can we reach first?
-   What exactly does each package include?
-   What are the price, timeline, and revision limits?
-   What examples can we show honestly?
-   What countries can we legally and operationally serve?
-   Which payment provider supports our business setup?
-   How will customers submit assets and receive final work?
-   What evidence would justify expanding the service menu?

Do not delay indefinitely trying to answer everything perfectly.
Validate the most important assumptions with prospective customers, then
build the smallest credible version.

## 16. Final recommendation

**Launch AdSpolisher as a service business first.**

The first website should help customers understand the offer, see
evidence of quality, request a quote, and purchase a well-defined
service. Use AI internally to improve speed and consistency, while
retaining human review.

Start with two strong, repeatable services---likely **Ad Audit &
Improvement** and **Ad Creative & Copy Package**---if those match the
team's actual capabilities. Offer larger marketing projects through
custom quotes. Add campaign management or recurring retainers only when
the team can fulfill them reliably.

Do not build a subscription-based AI platform at this stage. First prove
that customers will pay for the work, learn which services they
repeatedly need, and establish a reliable delivery process. The product
and technology can grow from that evidence.
