# AdSpolisher — Pre-Build Business & Product Research

Research date: October 25, 2026 · Assumption: India-first launch, with room to expand globally

The goal is to turn AdSpolisher into a real, monetizable business, not just an attractive AI website.

Before writing code, we need to answer six questions:

1. Who exactly will pay for this product?
2. What problem are we solving better than existing tools?
3. What will customers actually receive?
4. How will the business make money?
5. How will we find the first 10 paying customers?
6. What should we validate before investing in development?

My initial recommendation is to start with a focused AI-assisted ad improvement and auditing platform for small e-commerce brands, rather than trying to build an all-in-one advertising platform from day one.

### What does this mean for AdSpolisher?

It suggests a large market exists for businesses trying to acquire customers through online advertising. However, the total advertising spend is not AdSpolisher's addressable software market. We still need evidence that a defined group of advertisers has a problem they will pay us to solve.

Our opportunity hypothesis is:

> Small businesses are willing to advertise, but many struggle to determine what is weak about their ad, how to improve it, and what to test next without paying for extensive agency support.

That is the hypothesis we need to validate before building.

## 2. Define exactly what AdSpolisher does

The phrase “improve ads to grow sales” is a good starting vision, but it is too broad for an initial product.

A person might interpret it as generating ad images, writing copy, managing Meta Ads, finding audiences, analyzing ROAS, making landing pages, or managing complete advertising campaigns.

Trying to do all these things in the first release would create a large product without a clear reason for customers to choose it.

### Recommended positioning

Proposed positioning

# Turn rough ads into sharper, test-ready campaigns.

AdSpolisher analyzes an existing ad and its destination page, identifies specific weaknesses, and recommends improvements with alternative versions that advertisers can test.

Diagnose

Find unclear messages, weak hooks, vague benefits, missing details, and potential ad-to-page mismatches.

Improve

Rewrite the message, sharpen the offer, and generate alternative creative directions.

Test

Recommend practical experiments and the metrics to monitor.

The key distinction is that AdSpolisher should not merely generate more advertising copy. It should explain what to change, why the change may help, and how to test whether it actually helps.

We should not promise guaranteed sales, lower acquisition costs, or a specific return on ad spend. Those outcomes also depend on the offer, audience, pricing, competition, landing page, tracking, and execution.

| Freelancers and marketing agencies | Need to review ads and create recommendations for multiple clients    | Higher potential spend per account                          | Second           |

### Our initial ideal customer profile (ICP)

## Indian D2C e-commerce brands

Proposed customer profile to validate, not an established market fact.

- Small or growing online brand selling physical products.
- Already running, or preparing to run, Meta ads on Facebook and Instagram.
- Does not have a dedicated in-house creative strategist.
- Frequently needs new messages, creative angles, or campaign variations.
- Has an existing product page or website that can be analyzed alongside the ad.

Why start with this group? E-commerce has clear products, prices, benefits, offers, and destination pages. That gives the product useful inputs to analyze and makes recommendations more specific.

Agencies could eventually become a stronger revenue segment because one customer might use AdSpolisher for several brands. But agency workflows often need collaboration, client workspaces, white-label reports, and more sophisticated permissions. We should validate the simpler use case first.

One important qualification: the best niche is also the one your friend can reach. If he already has relationships with local businesses or agencies, those relationships may be more valuable than selecting a theoretically attractive segment.

### What about ChatGPT and Canva?

They are important substitutes, too. A user can already ask an AI assistant to rewrite an ad or use a design tool to make a creative.

Therefore, simply building an interface around an AI text-generation API will be easy to imitate and difficult to monetize consistently.

### The differentiator we should test

AdSpolisher could combine four things in one simple workflow:

1\. An explainable ad audit

Show the weak element, supporting evidence, its likely significance, and a suggested fix.

2\. Ad-to-landing-page consistency

Check whether the ad's main promise, offer, product details, and call to action match what the customer encounters after clicking.

3\. A usable testing plan

Produce a small number of distinct versions, explain the hypothesis behind each one, and identify what the advertiser should measure.

4\. A small-business-friendly workflow

Start with copy, product information, a creative upload, and a destination URL. No complicated advertising-account setup should be required.

These are proposed differentiators, not features that competitors categorically lack. We need to test whether our particular implementation is useful enough for people to switch from existing tools.

### C. Premium: Expert-reviewed advertising improvement

Before we have sophisticated software, we can sell a service that combines automated assistance with a human review.

For example, a customer submits an ad, the product produces the preliminary analysis, and someone reviews the recommendations before delivering a client-ready report.

This could bring in early revenue while teaching us which recommendations customers find useful and which they ignore.

### What should the actual audit evaluate?

I recommend a transparent rubric with the following dimensions.

| Dimension          | Questions the tool should answer                                     |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hook               | Does the opening capture attention and communicate relevance?        |
| Audience fit       | Is it clear who the product is for?                                  |
| Benefit clarity    | Does the ad explain the customer's practical benefit?                |
| Offer              | Are the price, promotion, or terms understandable and supported?     |
| Differentiation    | Does it explain why someone might choose this product?               |
| Trust              | Are credible evidence and important product details provided?        |
| Call to action     | Is the next step specific and clear?                                 |
| Creative fit       | Is the message suitable for the chosen format and placement?         |
| Landing-page match | Does the destination support the same promise and offer?             |
| Policy risks       | Are there unsupported, misleading, or potentially restricted claims? |

The tool could calculate an Ad Readiness Score, with a breakdown showing how it was calculated.

A critical distinction: this would be a measure of content quality and readiness according to our rubric, not a prediction that an advertisement will convert or generate a particular return.

Google itself provides platform-specific advice for responsive search ads, including varied headlines, clear benefits, specific calls to action, and consistency between the ad and landing page.&#x20;

## 6. Business model: how will AdSpolisher make money?

My recommendation is a service-led SaaS model: validate demand by providing paid ad audits, then convert the repeatable parts into a subscription product.

We should not depend on advertising revenue from the website itself. The product should earn money from businesses that use it.

Revenue stream 1: One-time paid audits

## ₹1,499–₹3,999 per audit

Proposed starting price to test, not an established market rate.

A detailed report for a customer who wants help fixing an existing ad but does not need a recurring subscription.

Revenue stream 2: Monthly subscriptions

## ₹799–₹1,999/month

Proposed pricing hypothesis.

Recurring audits, new copy variations, saved history, exports, and brand-specific context for businesses running ads regularly.

Revenue stream 3: Agency and team plans

## ₹5,999+/month

Future pricing hypothesis.

Multiple client workspaces, shared reports, collaboration, and branded exports for marketing professionals.

These prices are hypotheses to test through interviews and actual purchases. They are not forecasts, nor are they derived from a representative survey of Indian customers.

### A sensible initial pricing structure

I would initially test one free plan, one paid subscription, and one expert-reviewed service.

| Plan          | Proposed offer                      | Purpose                                             |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Free          | One basic audit with limited output | Acquisition                                         |
| Starter       | ₹799/month, with 10 full audits     | Test recurring willingness to pay                   |
| Expert Review | ₹1,499+ per audit                   | Earn early service revenue and learn from customers |
| Agency        | Introduce later                     | Serve teams after the workflow is validated         |

We can adjust the limits and prices after testing. Audit counts are particularly important because AI inference, file processing, and support create variable costs.

### What is the subscription actually selling?

Not access to AI alone. Customers can already access general-purpose AI tools.

We need to sell a repeatable workflow that saves time and improves decision-making: the input form, structured diagnosis, business-specific context, saved reports, test planning, and convenient exports.

## 7. How do we get our first 10 paying customers?

This is one of the most important parts of the entire business.

A polished website does not automatically create demand. We need a route to customers before investing heavily in the product.

### Channel 1: Direct outreach to small brands

Make a list of 30–50 relevant e-commerce brands. Study their publicly available advertisements and product pages, then contact the owners or marketing managers with an offer to review one ad.

The outreach should be personalized. Instead of saying “We built an AI advertising platform,” identify a specific, observable issue and offer a sample recommendation.

Do not pretend to know that an ad is losing money based only on its appearance.

### Channel 2: A free, useful audit

Make the free audit the main call to action on the website.

A visitor submits an ad and product information, receives an immediate summary, and can purchase a detailed report or subscription. The free version should demonstrate the quality of the recommendations rather than simply produce generic AI copy.

### Channel 3: Freelancers and small agencies

Find professionals who manage advertising for several small businesses. Offer a pilot workflow where they submit an ad and receive a client-ready review.

This will also help us discover whether agencies are a better-paying customer segment than individual business owners.

### Channel 4: Public educational content

Publish genuinely useful ad teardowns, examples of stronger versus weaker messaging, and landing-page improvement guides.

The objective is to establish credibility and attract people already searching for advertising help. Content should support product acquisition rather than become a separate content-creation business.

### The first-customer offer

A practical first offer could be:

> Submit one existing ad and its destination page. Receive a detailed review, three improvement directions, and a testing plan. Join the initial paid pilot at an introductory price in exchange for detailed feedback.

The first customers should receive a result that a business owner could actually use, not just a high score and a wall of generated text.

## 8. Pre-build validation: what we must do before coding

This is where we should spend our first effort.

## 01

Interview 15 potential customers

Speak with e-commerce founders and performance marketers. Ask about the last time an ad did not perform as expected, how they diagnosed the problem, what tools they used, what it cost, and what they changed.

Avoid questions like “Would you use our AI?” People may say yes to be encouraging. Ask about actual past behavior and spend.

## 02

Analyze 5–10 real ads manually

With permission, review real ads alongside the product pages. Write the report by hand, using AI as an assistant if helpful.

Record which recommendations customers find useful, which they reject, and whether they make changes afterward.

## 03

Create a clickable prototype and sample report

Demonstrate the experience from submitting an ad to receiving recommendations. The prototype can be static at first; it does not require a working AI backend.

## 04

Try to sell a paid pilot

Offer the service to the interviewees who expressed a real need. Ask for payment, not just a promise to use the product someday.

A reasonable initial decision rule—not an industry benchmark—is to seek three paid pilot customers before committing to the full build.

## 05

Refine the product around repeated needs

If customers repeatedly request ad-to-landing-page checks and alternative copy variations, those become core features. If their biggest need is campaign analytics, reconsider the scope before building.

### What questions should we ask during interviews?

These six questions will uncover more useful information than asking whether someone likes the product idea.

1. Show me an ad you recently created or ran. How did you decide what to write or design?
2. Tell me about the last time an ad performed below expectations. What did you do next?
3. How do you currently create and test different ad versions?
4. What advertising, design, AI, or agency tools do you already pay for?
5. Which part of improving an ad takes the most time, and what happens when you get it wrong?
6. Would you pay for a detailed review of a real ad today? What would the report need to contain to justify the price?

After the interview, offer a sample audit or paid pilot. Actual adoption and payment are stronger evidence than positive feedback.

## 9. MVP scope: what should we actually build?

Once validation confirms the need, we can move from manual audits to a working web application.

The MVP should solve one problem well: analyze an existing advertisement and make the next version easier to improve and test.

### The initial user journey

Step 1 — Provide context

Platform, campaign objective, product, target audience, and offer

Step 2 — Submit the existing ad

Paste copy, optionally upload a creative, and provide the landing-page URL

Step 3 — Receive the audit

A transparent score, prioritized weaknesses, explanations, and recommended fixes

Step 4 — Generate improvements

Three alternative versions with different messaging approaches

Step 5 — Test and review

A test hypothesis, suggested metric, and saved report

### Suggested release priorities

P0

Must have in the first release

Ad input form, structured audit, transparent scoring rubric, three copy variations, actionable recommendations, report history, and a simple export or copy function.

P1

After the basic workflow works

Better creative-image analysis, saved brand voice, project management, more detailed landing-page checks, and customer-provided performance metrics.

Later

After proven demand

Meta or Google account integrations, campaign analytics, team collaboration, agency workspaces, image/video generation, and advanced experiment tracking.

### What we should deliberately avoid building first

Do not start with an automated campaign launcher, complex budget optimization, predictive ROAS, or integrations with every advertising platform. These features add significant complexity, permissions, costs, and potential consequences if recommendations are wrong.

Also, do not make the initial product entirely dependent on ad-account connections. Users should be able to test its core value without granting access to sensitive business accounts.

### A practical architecture

Given that the project can start as a small web product, a TypeScript-based stack would be sufficient:

| Layer          | Initial approach                                                             |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Frontend       | Next.js, React, and TypeScript                                               |
| Backend        | Next.js server-side endpoints or a small dedicated Node.js API               |
| Database       | PostgreSQL with Prisma                                                       |
| Authentication | Introduce when saving reports and managing paid accounts requires it         |
| AI analysis    | A structured LLM API workflow, with validated output schemas                 |
| Payments       | A payment provider appropriate for the initial market                        |
| Storage        | Secure storage for user-uploaded creative assets, if needed                  |
| Monitoring     | Error tracking, usage tracking, API-cost monitoring, and feedback collection |

The exact stack can change; the business hypothesis should not depend on a particular framework.

Use deterministic checks where possible and AI for interpretation and recommendations. For example, the system can validate required inputs and supported output formats with ordinary code, while an AI model interprets the message and suggests alternative wording.

Keep the original claims and supplied facts as context so that the model does not invent testimonials, discounts, product features, certifications, or performance statistics.

## 11. Risks, trust, and legal considerations

This needs to be part of the product design from the beginning.

### Misleading advertising claims

Google Ads policies prohibit misleading representations and false or unrealistic claims.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://support.google.com\&sz=32)

Advertising Policies Help

+1



For AdSpolisher, that means the system should not casually generate claims like “guaranteed 10x sales,” fake customer testimonials, fabricated before-and-after results, or unsupported medical and financial claims.

We should distinguish a suggestion from a verified fact, flag claims that need evidence, and explain that a policy-risk check does not guarantee ad approval.

### Privacy and business data

Ad copy, product information, uploaded creatives, campaign reports, and connected advertising accounts can contain confidential business information. Use data minimization, access controls, secure storage, clear retention and deletion rules, and transparent disclosures about AI processing.

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, and the associated Act have a phased implementation timeline. Some provisions begin taking effect in November 2026, with other substantive provisions scheduled later. The official notification and timeline should be reviewed before launch, alongside applicable contractual and privacy requirements.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.meity.gov.in\&sz=32)

MeitY

+1



Practical defaults for the MVP:

- Do not require advertising-account credentials.
- Avoid collecting customer lists or unnecessary personal data.
- State what information is sent to AI providers and how it is handled.
- Provide deletion controls and a defined retention policy.
- Review platform API terms and permissions before adding integrations.

### AI quality and customer trust

A generic rewrite is easy to produce, but specific, reliable advice is harder.

For each recommendation, show the issue, the evidence or input it relies on, the proposed change, and any uncertainty. When the system lacks relevant information, it should ask for it or explain the limitation rather than invent context.

We should test the quality of the audit against a set of human-reviewed examples before using the results to charge customers. The scoring rubric should be stable enough that an improvement in score means something consistent.

### Brand and domain checks

Before investing in a visual identity, check the spelling, relevant trademarks, social handles, existing businesses, and app-store names. A domain purchase by itself does not establish trademark availability.

Use AdsPolisher consistently in the visible brand identity if that is the chosen spelling, while retaining the domain already purchased.

## 12. A 30-day plan before committing to the full product

Here is the sequence I would follow. The purpose is to discover whether the business deserves the investment before writing a large amount of code.

## Week 1

Research

Understand the customer and competitors

Interview potential users, compare competing tools, collect representative ads and product pages with permission, and write down the top recurring problems.

Deliverable: An initial customer profile, competitor comparison, and prioritized problem statement.

## Week 2

Prototype

Design the audit and the user journey

Create a sample report, test the rubric on real examples, and build a clickable prototype or simple landing page with a clear call to action.

Deliverable: A demonstrable product concept and an audit format users can evaluate.

## Week 3

Pilot

Deliver audits manually and test pricing

Offer the initial service to prospective customers. Measure whether they understand the recommendations, implement them, and pay for the output.

Deliverable: Pilot feedback, willingness-to-pay evidence, and a more focused offer.

## Week 4

Decision

Decide whether and what to build

Look for repeated customer needs, willingness to pay, and a report that reliably delivers useful recommendations. Select only the essential features for the first software release.

Deliverable: A validated MVP scope, initial pricing hypothesis, and development backlog—or a revised proposition if the evidence is weak.

## 13. How will we know the business is working?

Measure more than website visits and the number of ads generated.

| Metric                                      | What it tells us                           |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Visitors to audit submissions               | Whether our offer is compelling            |
| Time to first useful result                 | Whether the product is easy to use         |
| Recommendations accepted or implemented     | Whether the audit is useful                |
| Free users who become paying customers      | Whether the value justifies the price      |
| Repeat usage and subscription cancellations | Whether customers have a recurring problem |
| Variable cost per audit                     | Whether the product can be profitable      |
| Customer feedback on real campaign results  | Whether the product is helping in practice |

Where possible, compare customers' actual results before and after changes, while recognizing that ad performance can be affected by many other variables. We should not claim causation based on a simple before-and-after comparison.

## 14. The final business decision

Here is the direction I recommend for AdSpolisher.

## Initial business blueprint

Customer

Indian small and growing e-commerce brands that already run paid ads.

Platform

Start with Meta advertising creatives and their destination pages.

Core product

A structured ad audit, specific improvements, three testable versions, and an experiment plan.

Revenue

Paid audit services first, followed by a monthly subscription if customers need repeated use.

Acquisition

Direct outreach, free sample audits, agency partnerships, and useful advertising guides.

MVP

No mandatory ad-account integration, no campaign automation, and no unsupported sales predictions.

First milestone

Three paid pilot customers and evidence that the audit solves a recurring problem.
