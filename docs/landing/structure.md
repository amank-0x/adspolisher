landing page structure

1. Header / Navigation
Essential
Logo, Services, Our Work, How It Works, Pricing, FAQ, and a standout Get a Quote button. Put Sign In in a secondary position if you need accounts.


2. Hero — Your main selling point
Essential
Make the headline outcome-focused. Use a short supporting paragraph and two CTAs.

Example headline: “Better Ads. Stronger Brands. More Growth.”

Supporting text: “From scroll-stopping creatives to smarter ad campaigns, we help businesses turn ideas into marketing that works.”

Buttons: Explore Services · Start a Project

Visual: a polished collage of ad creatives, campaign mockups, or brand assets.


3. Trust / Social Proof

Help visitors feel confident before they scroll further.

Show real client logos, verified project counts, industries served, or measurable campaign results—with permission and evidence.

If you're new, use your team's experience, sample projects, or a transparent portfolio instead of invented statistics.


4. Services — What exactly do you do?
Essential
This is one of the most important sections. Use 3–4 clear service cards:
- Ad Creative & Design — Visuals for social media, display ads, and campaigns.
- Ad Copywriting — Headlines, hooks, and persuasive ad copy.
- Ad Audit & Optimization — Review existing ads and recommend improvements.
- Campaign Strategy — Audience, messaging, and creative direction.
Each card should explain the deliverable and link to its service details or quote form.


5. Creative Showcase / Portfolio
Essential
Let people see the quality of your work rather than simply promising it.

Display sample campaigns, before-and-after improvements, ad variations, and short case studies.

If these are concept projects rather than paid client work, label them clearly as concept work.


6. How It Works

Explain the customer journey in four simple steps:
1. Tell us your goal — Product, audience, budget, and objectives.
2. Choose your service — Select a package or request a custom solution.
3. We create — AI-assisted production with human review and refinement.
4. Review and receive — Approve the deliverables and receive the final assets.
This reduces uncertainty about what happens after someone clicks “Start a Project.”


7. Why AdSpolisher?

Explain your differentiation rather than using generic claims.
- AI-assisted speed with human quality control.
- Creative work tailored to each business.
- Clear deliverables and project scope.
- A straightforward process from brief to delivery.
- One place to access your selected marketing services.
Only promise capabilities you can consistently deliver.


8. Pricing / Packages
Recommended
Give visitors an idea of what working with you costs. Start with simple service packages, for example:
- Ad Audit — fixed price for reviewing existing ads.
- Creative Package — a defined number of ad creatives and copy variations.
- Custom Campaign — tailored scope and quotation.
Show what's included, turnaround expectations, revision limits, and the CTA for each package. If the price depends on scope, say “Starting at…” or “Request a quote.”


9. Reviews / Testimonials

Use genuine customer feedback, ideally with permission, a name or business attribution, and relevant context.

If you don't have customers yet, skip this section for now. You can replace it with “What to expect” or a sample project breakdown until you have real testimonials.


10. FAQ

Answer the questions that might stop a visitor from starting:
- What services does AdSpolisher provide?
- Do you work with businesses internationally?
- How does AI fit into your creative process?
- Can I request a custom package?
- How many revisions are included?
- How long does a project take?
- Do you guarantee ad performance?
- How do payments and project approvals work?
Keep answers short and honest. Avoid promising guaranteed results.


11. Final CTA — Convert interested visitors

After explaining your value, ask the visitor to take the next step.

Example headline: “Ready to make your next campaign count?”

Supporting text: “Tell us what you're building. We'll help you find the right creative solution.”

Button: Start Your Project


12. Footer

Include the logo and short description, service links, portfolio, contact details, social links, privacy policy, terms of service, and copyright information.



Should you include “All-in-One Marketplace”?
I'd be careful with that phrase. It creates the expectation that customers can browse a range of providers, compare offers, and purchase services through a marketplace.

For AdSpolisher's current service-led model, these alternatives are more accurate:
Option A · Best for your current model

“Your all-in-one partner for better advertising.”

Positions AdSpolisher as a single marketing partner that handles multiple services.


Option B · Best for a creative-focused brand

“Everything you need to create ads that stand out.”

Focuses on creative output and is easy to understand.


Option C · Best if you eventually build a marketplace

“One platform. Every creative solution your brand needs.”

Use this when the product actually supports a broader self-service or marketplace experience.


# Recommended page order for AdSpolisher
This is the order I'd actually build—not just a list of possible sections:

Navigation and primary CTA


02 Hero

What you do and who you help


03 Trust strip

Proof or credible experience


04 Services

What customers can buy


05 Portfolio

Show the quality of your work


06 How it works

Make the process clear


07 Why AdSpolisher

Explain the difference


08 Pricing

Help customers assess affordability


09 Testimonials

Real customer experiences, when available


10 FAQ

Resolve objections


11 Final CTA

Get a quote or start a project


12 Footer

Contact, navigation and legal links



One important recommendation
Don't make the landing page a giant wall of marketing claims. Make every section answer one customer question:
- Hero: What can you do for me?
- Services: What can I purchase?
- Portfolio: Can you produce good work?
- Process: How does this work?
- Pricing: Can I afford it?
- Reviews: Can I trust you?
- FAQ: What else do I need to know?
- CTA: How do I get started?
